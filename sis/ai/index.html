Program Overview

AI Creative Media Academy teaches students to direct AI instead of being replaced by it. Students turn their own idea into a short vertical video, learning how to write prompts, direct voice, create visuals, animate images, and edit, while thinking carefully about honesty, privacy, and responsible use.

Program Objectives

  • Explain what AI is, what it can do, and its limits, including that AI can be wrong or biased.
  • Write clear prompts with context, goal, and instructions.
  • Write a short story script with a beginning, middle, and ending.
  • Direct a voice-over through character, emotion, and pacing.
  • Plan scenes with a storyboard and keep visuals consistent.
  • Turn images into short videos with motion and transitions.
  • Edit audio, subtitles, and thumbnail, and present the work.
  • Use AI honestly, safely, and with credit.

10-Session SyllabusSilabus 10 Sesi

01What Is AI? & Responsible Use

Learning outcome: Students explain what AI can and cannot do, and agree on rules for using it honestly and safely.

Session flow

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Opening: AI Detective Quiz warm-up (Kahoot).
15:20–15:30Teacher demo: How AI works in simple terms, with examples of good and wrong AI answers.
15:30–15:40Guided practice: Sort examples into human idea, human decision, and AI help.
15:40–16:05Creative production: Write a personal AI Promise: safe, honest, responsible.
16:05–16:10Reflection & saving files: Share one rule; create a personal project folder.

Output

AI Promise card and project folder.

02Story Idea & Script with AI

Learning outcome: Students choose a useful story idea and write a short script with a hook, middle, and closing.

Session flow

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Opening: Spark ideas from safe themes: kindness, environment, school life, local culture.
15:20–15:30Teacher demo: Turn one broad topic into a short video idea and a 5–7 sentence script.
15:30–15:40Guided practice: Co-write a sample script with an AI assistant and edit it together.
15:40–16:05Creative production: Write and improve a personal script for a 20–40 second video.
16:05–16:10Reflection & saving files: Read the script aloud; mark parts that are too long or unclear.

Output

Final script for the student's own video.

03Voice Direction & AI Voice-over

Learning outcome: Students direct a voice through character, emotion, and pacing, and compare two voice-over takes.

Session flow

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Opening: Listen to two voices reading the same line; discuss the difference.
15:20–15:30Teacher demo: Voice Card: character, emotion, speed, pauses.
15:30–15:40Guided practice: Write a Voice Card for the sample script.
15:40–16:05Creative production: Create a Voice Card for the own script; teacher generates two takes to compare.
16:05–16:10Reflection & saving files: Choose the best take and save the audio file.

Output

Voice Card and selected voice-over.

04Scene Breakdown & Storyboard

Learning outcome: Students split a script into 4–6 scenes, each with a visual world and an emotional meaning.

Session flow

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Opening: Watch a short clip and count its scenes.
15:20–15:30Teacher demo: How a storyboard plans scenes before any visuals are made.
15:30–15:40Guided practice: Break the sample script into scenes together.
15:40–16:05Creative production: Build a storyboard: dialogue, visual world, and feeling for every scene.
16:05–16:10Reflection & saving files: Check that the story is simple enough to produce.

Output

Completed storyboard.

05Visual Prompt Formula & Style Bible

Learning outcome: Students write clear image prompts and keep characters and style consistent across scenes.

Session flow

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Opening: Guess what prompt created a shown image.
15:20–15:30Teacher demo: Prompt formula: subject, setting, lighting, composition, style.
15:30–15:40Guided practice: Improve a weak prompt step by step.
15:40–16:05Creative production: Create a Style Bible and prompts for every scene.
16:05–16:10Reflection & saving files: Peer check: are the prompts clear and consistent?

Output

Style Bible and scene prompts.

06AI Image Generation (Teacher-Guided)

Learning outcome: Students generate scene visuals, choose the best results, and revise prompts when output is off.

Session flow

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Opening: Recap the Style Bible; safety check on topics and content.
15:20–15:30Teacher demo: Teacher generates one scene live and explains choosing and rejecting results.
15:30–15:40Guided practice: Revise one prompt together after a poor result.
15:40–16:05Creative production: Generate and select the main image for each scene with teacher help.
16:05–16:10Reflection & saving files: Rename and organise image files.

Output

One selected visual for every scene.

07Image to Video & Transitions (Teacher-Guided)

Learning outcome: Students bring still images to life with motion, camera movement, and transitions between scenes.

Session flow

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Opening: Compare a still image with the same image animated.
15:20–15:30Teacher demo: Motion prompts, camera moves, and transition styles.
15:30–15:40Guided practice: Animate one sample scene together.
15:40–16:05Creative production: Create motion clips for the scenes with teacher help; check characters, text, and symbols first.
16:05–16:10Reflection & saving files: Save clips and note which ones need revision.

Output

Animated clip for every scene.

08Sound, Music & Editing in Canva

Learning outcome: Students edit a vertical 9:16 video with the voice-over as the backbone, plus sound effects and subtitles.

Session flow

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Opening: Watch a silent video, then the same video with sound.
15:20–15:30Teacher demo: Canva basics: timeline, voice-over backbone, subtitles, ambience.
15:30–15:40Guided practice: Import scenes and place the voice-over together.
15:40–16:05Creative production: Edit the full video with subtitles and sound effects.
16:05–16:10Reflection & saving files: Export a draft and save the project.

Output

Draft edited video.

09Thumbnail, Caption & Peer Review

Learning outcome: Students give and use feedback with a simple rubric, and plan safe, honest sharing of their work.

Session flow

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Opening: Quick review of rules from the AI Promise.
15:20–15:30Teacher demo: Feedback rubric; how to write a thumbnail and caption; crediting AI help.
15:30–15:40Guided practice: Review a sample video with the rubric.
15:40–16:05Creative production: Revise the video from peer feedback; make a thumbnail and caption.
16:05–16:10Reflection & saving files: Teacher checkpoint: subtitles, audio, AI credit, file name.

Output

Final video, thumbnail, and caption.

10AI Expo Showcase & Graduation

Learning outcome: Students present their video, explain their workflow and creative decisions, and reflect on responsible AI use.

Session flow

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Opening: Set up the exhibition and rehearse the presentation.
15:20–15:30Teacher demo: Presentation tips: workflow, tools, decisions, and ethics.
15:30–15:40Guided practice: Practise a 1-minute explanation with a partner.
15:40–16:05Creative production: Showcase videos to classmates, with parents where invited; explain the process.
16:05–16:10Reflection & saving files: Reflection and certificate ceremony.

Output

Presented final video and certificate.

Tools & Digital Safety

Tools

  • ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini: ideas, scripts, prompts, and feedback.
  • Google AI Studio: voice-over (teacher-guided).
  • Image generator and image-to-video tools such as Dola and Dreamina (teacher-guided).
  • Canva: editing, subtitles, thumbnail, and presentation.

Digital safety

  • Generating is done with school or teacher accounts, following each platform's age rules; students do not need personal AI accounts.
  • No personal data, face photos, full names, voices, or family information goes into public AI tools.
  • Student work is shared only after teacher and, when needed, parent approval.
  • Students always check AI answers and credit AI help.
