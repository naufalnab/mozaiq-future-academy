Program Overview

Flag Football is a non-tackling version of American Football. Instead of tackling a ball carrier, a defender stops the play by removing one of the runner’s flags attached to a waist belt. The program introduces students to the sport through progressive, inclusive and game-based learning.

The syllabus balances fundamental movement and ball skills with decision-making, teamwork, communication and sportsmanship. Activities progress from individual skills to partner work, small-sided scenarios and a final festival.

Program Objectives

  • Understand the purpose, basic rules, equipment and safe-play expectations of Flag Football.
  • Develop throwing, catching, running, agility and flag-pulling fundamentals.
  • Recognize basic offensive roles, defensive positioning and simple passing routes.
  • Apply communication, spacing, teamwork and decision-making in small-sided games.
  • Demonstrate respect, fair play, confidence and positive participation.

Detailed Lesson Plans

Each lesson below follows the same 55-minute schedule. Coaches may adjust distances, ball size, team numbers and rules to suit student age, ability, space and school policy.

10-Session SyllabusSilabus 10 Sesi

01Introduction To Flag Football

Learning outcome: Students identify the basic equipment and objective of Flag Football, follow stop/start signals and demonstrate safe running and evasion.

A. Technical Learning – 15:15 to 15:42

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Dynamic warm-up: light jog, mobility, skipping and movement games.
15:20–15:25Coach introduction: explain the aim of the game, no-tackling rule and how a flag pull ends a play.
15:25–15:31Equipment discovery: football, flag belt, cones, boundaries and end zones; demonstrate correct belt fit.
15:31–15:37Athletic stance and movement: ready position, start/stop, change of direction and spatial awareness.
15:37–15:42Guided practice: players move through cone lanes while responding to coach signals.

B. Games & Application – 15:42 to 16:10

TimeActivity
15:42–15:47Water break, belt check, divide into teams and explain game boundaries.
15:47–15:55Flag Tag: one or two taggers try to remove a flag; players evade without pushing or holding.
15:55–16:03Run & Escape: runners carry a ball through a marked lane while defenders attempt a safe flag pull.
16:03–16:10Modified mini-game: simple end-zone running game; finish with quick reflection and equipment count.

Equipment: Junior footballs, flag belts, cones, bibs and whistle.

02Passing Fundamentals

Learning outcome: Students demonstrate a balanced throwing stance, grip the ball correctly and complete short partner passes with improving control.

A. Technical Learning – 15:15 to 15:42

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Dynamic warm-up with shoulder mobility, arm circles, trunk rotation and light jogging.
15:20–15:25Theory and demonstration: grip, non-throwing hand support, side-on stance, step toward target and follow-through.
15:25–15:31Shadow throwing: rehearse the throwing motion without a ball, then with a soft/junior football.
15:31–15:37Partner passing: short-distance chest-height passes; focus on ready hands and accuracy.
15:37–15:42Progression: move back gradually and aim at numbered cone/target gates.

B. Games & Application – 15:42 to 16:10

TimeActivity
15:42–15:47Hydration, collect balls and set safe throwing lanes with adequate spacing.
15:47–15:55Target Throwing Challenge: teams score by passing through target gates or hitting safe ground targets.
15:55–16:03Passing Accuracy Competition: pairs earn points for consecutive accurate passes at varied distances.
16:03–16:10Partner Passing Relay and recap: celebrate control and accuracy rather than throwing power.

Equipment: Junior footballs, cones/target gates, bibs and whistle.

03Catching & Ball Control

Learning outcome: Students track the ball, show an appropriate hand position and catch stationary and moving passes with increasing confidence.

A. Technical Learning – 15:15 to 15:42

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Warm-up: jogging, lateral movement, hand-eye coordination and reaction cues.
15:20–15:25Demonstration: watch the ball, present hands, absorb the catch and secure the ball to the body.
15:25–15:31Stationary partner catches from short distances; progress from gentle underhand tosses to controlled throws.
15:31–15:37Moving catches: jog into a marked receiving zone, turn to the passer and catch on the move.
15:37–15:42Catch-and-secure drill: catch, tuck the ball safely and accelerate through a short cone lane.

B. Games & Application – 15:42 to 16:10

TimeActivity
15:42–15:47Water break and set up receiving zones; remind students not to dive or collide.
15:47–15:55Catch & Run: receive a pass inside a zone and run to an end marker.
15:55–16:03Moving Target Catch: receivers move between gates while partners pass to open space.
16:03–16:103-Pass Team Challenge: teams complete three controlled passes before crossing a scoring line.

Equipment: Junior footballs, cones, bibs and whistle.

04Running, Agility & Flag Pulling

Learning outcome: Students change direction under control, evade a defender and demonstrate a safe flag-pulling technique without body contact.

A. Technical Learning – 15:15 to 15:42

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Warm-up: dynamic leg movements, short accelerations and controlled deceleration.
15:20–15:25Theory/demo: safe defensive approach, eyes on the runner’s hip area, reach for the flag and avoid grabbing clothing/body.
15:25–15:31Agility ladder or cone patterns: forward, lateral, zigzag and stop-start movement.
15:31–15:37Flag-pull technique in pairs at walking speed, then controlled jogging; reset after each attempt.
15:37–15:421v1 lane practice: runner uses a change of direction; defender takes a safe angle to the flag.

B. Games & Application – 15:42 to 16:10

TimeActivity
15:42–15:47Hydration, check flag belts and mark clear one-way lanes.
15:47–15:55Flag Stealing Challenge: players collect a flag from a moving opponent in a defined space.
15:55–16:03Zigzag Escape Race: runners navigate cones while a defender attempts a legal flag pull.
16:03–16:101v1 Flag Pull Challenge: short rounds with coach feedback; no tackling, pushing or blocking.

Equipment: Flag belts, cones/agility markers, bibs and whistle.

05Offensive Skills & Route Running

Learning outcome: Students identify quarterback and receiver roles, run simple routes and connect a pass with a moving receiver.

A. Technical Learning – 15:15 to 15:42

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Warm-up: dynamic movement, acceleration and light partner passing.
15:20–15:25Explain offense: quarterback, receiver, spacing and moving into open space.
15:25–15:31Route demonstration and walk-through: Go (straight), Slant (diagonal), Out (toward sideline) and Hook (stop/turn back).
15:31–15:37Route running without a ball; receivers focus on direction, timing and looking back to the passer.
15:37–15:42Quarterback-receiver practice: short passes on a selected route, rotating roles.

B. Games & Application – 15:42 to 16:10

TimeActivity
15:42–15:47Water break and assign passing pairs/lanes.
15:47–15:55Route Running Challenge: teams earn points for completing a route and reaching the correct cone.
15:55–16:03Quarterback & Receiver Competition: complete passes on different routes from a safe distance.
16:03–16:10Pass and Score Relay: each team completes a route-and-catch sequence before the next pair starts.

Equipment: Junior footballs, cones, flag belts, bibs and whistle.

06Defensive Skills & Teamwork

Learning outcome: Students adopt a balanced defensive stance, track a ball carrier, choose a safe pursuit angle and cooperate with teammates.

A. Technical Learning – 15:15 to 15:42

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Warm-up: lateral shuffles, backpedal-to-run transitions and mobility.
15:20–15:25Theory/demo: defender responsibilities, staying between runner and end zone, and avoiding contact.
15:25–15:31Defensive footwork: mirror a partner’s lateral and forward movement while maintaining safe distance.
15:31–15:37Pursuit-angle drill: approach a moving runner from an angle and attempt a controlled flag pull.
15:37–15:422-defender communication: call 'left', 'right' or 'help' and maintain balanced coverage.

B. Games & Application – 15:42 to 16:10

TimeActivity
15:42–15:47Hydration and explain defender/runner rotations.
15:47–15:55Defender vs Runner: short one-on-one rounds with a clear start and finish line.
15:55–16:03Protect the End Zone: defenders work together to prevent a runner entering a marked scoring area.
16:03–16:102v2 Defensive Challenge: teams communicate and attempt legal flag pulls; rotate roles.

Equipment: Flag belts, footballs, cones/end-zone markers, bibs and whistle.

Learning outcome: Students understand simple team formations, use spacing and communicate a basic play before executing it.

TimeActivity
15:15–15:20Warm-up: team movement relay and dynamic stretching.
15:20–15:25Explain basic roles and simple formations; introduce a visual play diagram using cones.
15:25–15:31Spacing exercise: players spread out, identify open lanes and avoid crowding teammates.
15:31–15:37Play-call walk-through: assign roles and rehearse a simple route combination at walking pace.
15:37–15:42Team practice: run the selected play at controlled speed, then switch roles.

B. Games & Application – 15:42 to 16:10

TimeActivity
15:42–15:47Water break; teams select a simple play name and review safe-play rules.
15:47–15:55Create Your Own Play: small groups design and demonstrate one simple passing play.
15:55–16:03Team Strategy Challenge: teams score for completing a coordinated play and communicating clearly.

B. Games & Application – 15:42 to 16:10

TimeActivity

Safety Guidelines & Risk Management

  1. No tackling, pushing, tripping, charging, holding, or intentional body contact. Flag Football is a non-tackling activity.
  2. Defenders must attempt to remove a flag only; they must not grab clothing, arms, waist or any part of the body.
  3. No diving, sliding into players, or jumping into crowded spaces to make a play.
  4. Fit flag belts correctly and ensure flags are visible and detachable before play begins.
  5. Use age-appropriate footballs, manageable throwing distances and clear one-way drill lanes.
  6. Keep adequate spacing between passing groups; students retrieve balls only when the coach signals.
  7. Begin each session with a dynamic warm-up and finish with a brief cool-down/reflection as time allows.
  8. The coach must explain the stop signal. All players stop immediately when the whistle or agreed signal is given.
  9. Check the field for holes, wet/slippery areas, debris, hard objects and other hazards before activity.
  10. Provide water breaks and monitor signs of heat stress, fatigue, discomfort or injury.
  11. Stop outdoor activity during lightning, heavy rain, unsafe heat conditions or any condition identified by SIS as unsafe.
  12. Follow SIS safeguarding, supervision, emergency response, incident reporting and first-aid procedures. Report injuries promptly to the designated school contact.
