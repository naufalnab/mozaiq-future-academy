Program Overview

The Mozaiq Future Academy Archery Academy introduces students to the fundamentals of archery through structured technical instruction, guided practice, creative target activities, and friendly competition. The program is designed to be engaging and progressive, helping students develop safe equipment-handling habits alongside focus, coordination, confidence, discipline, and sportsmanship.

Program Objectives

  • Understand essential archery range rules and safe shooting procedures.
  • Identify and handle beginner archery equipment responsibly.
  • Develop basic stance, posture, grip, drawing, aiming, release, and follow-through skills.
  • Improve hand-eye coordination, balance, concentration, and consistency.
  • Experience creative target formats, including flip-flop, spinning, and moving targets, when suitable and approved.
  • Build confidence, resilience, teamwork, and positive sporting behaviour.
  • Apply learned skills in a supervised mini championship.

Standard Session Structure

Each session follows a consistent 55-minute structure. The technical segment includes warm-up, theory, demonstration, and skill drills. The applied segment uses games and challenges to reinforce the session's learning objectives.

01Introduction To Archery & Range Safety

Session focus: Understand the sport, equipment, range layout, and essential safety commands.

Technical learning & drilling

  • Introduction to archery and its basic principles.
  • Identify the bow, arrows, target face, quiver, arm guard, and finger protection.
  • Learn the waiting line, shooting line, target line, and safe retrieval procedure.
  • Practise coach-led safety commands and basic ready / stop positions.
  • Introduce a comfortable, balanced standing position without rushing into shooting.

Games & application

  • Target Discovery: identify target colours and scoring zones from the waiting area.
  • Safety Signals Game: respond correctly to coach instructions and demonstrate safe movement.
  • If range checks are complete, students may take supervised introductory shots at a close, suitable distance.

Learning outcome: Students can identify key equipment, explain basic range rules, and follow the coach's commands.

Technical learning & drilling

  • Review safety commands and the shooting sequence.
  • Practise foot placement, open stance, posture, and body alignment.
  • Understand relaxed shoulders and balanced weight distribution.
  • Use simple body-position checkpoints before drawing.
  • Perform controlled, coach-supervised practice at an appropriate distance.

Games & application

  • Balance & Stance Challenge: hold a stable archery stance for a short, comfortable interval.
  • Stance Relay: teams demonstrate the correct sequence of foot placement and posture without racing with equipment.
  • Optional target round: earn points for following the full setup routine, not only for hitting the target.

Learning outcome: Students demonstrate a stable stance and can identify key posture checkpoints.

Technical learning & drilling

  • Explain the purpose of a repeatable anchor point.
  • Practise shoulder alignment and controlled drawing using coach-approved methods.
  • Understand the difference between muscular control and unnecessary tension.
  • Use a safe, low-load demonstration or training aid where appropriate.

Games & application

  • Technique Detective: identify good posture and alignment in coach demonstrations.
  • Optional target challenge rewards a consistent routine rather than speed.

Learning outcome: Students understand drawing mechanics and can demonstrate a repeatable anchor reference appropriate to their equipment.

Games & application

  • Shot Sequence Relay: teams place the steps of the shooting sequence in the correct order.
  • Consistency Round: students compare their own grouping and reflect on technique.

Learning outcome: Students understand the full shooting sequence and demonstrate improved control and follow-through.

Session focus: Consolidate basic accuracy and introduce a two-face / reversible target format.

Technical learning & drilling

  • Review range safety and the complete shooting sequence.
  • Practise grouping and accuracy on a standard, stationary target first.
  • Introduce the flip-flop target format and explain how the target face changes between rounds.
  • Demonstrate target reset procedures: all changes occur only after the range is declared clear.
  • Students practise on one stable target face at a time.

Games & application

  • Flip-Flop Target Challenge: alternate between two target faces or scoring layouts between safe rounds.
  • Alternating Target Competition: score points on each face after the coach has confirmed the target is correctly set and secured.
  • Accuracy Ladder: progress through achievable scoring goals.

Learning outcome: Students improve consistency and adapt to different target layouts while following safe reset procedures.

Special safety condition: The target must be stationary, securely locked, and checked before shooting. No target may be flipped, turned, or adjusted while anyone is on the shooting line or while the range is active.

Technical learning & drilling

  • Review static-target accuracy and range commands.
  • Introduce spinning-target and moving-target designs, their components, and safe operating zones.
  • Discuss observation, timing, and visual tracking as concepts; do not encourage students to rush a shot.
  • Demonstrate the mechanism with bows down and the range closed.
  • If the equipment, risk assessment, and school approval permit, the coach may run a controlled demonstration or supervised activity using a purpose-built archery target system.

Games & application

  • Spinning Target Observation: students observe a target mechanism and identify safe start / stop signals.
  • Dynamic Target Game: use a non-shooting visual tracking activity or a coach-approved target demonstration.
  • If approved for shooting, use only a purpose-built, secured target system, controlled speed, clear backstop, and a coach-managed firing sequence.

Learning outcome: Students understand dynamic target concepts, demonstrate observation skills, and follow additional controls for specialist equipment.

Special safety condition: Dynamic shooting is optional and subject to a documented risk assessment, equipment suitability, school approval, adequate range containment, and qualified instructor supervision. If any condition is not met, the session remains a static-target or non-shooting demonstration.

Technical learning & drilling

  • Review safe shooting routines and precision fundamentals.
  • Explain the difference between a standard target challenge and a specialist trickshoot demonstration.
  • Introduce creative target placement using safe, fixed targets within the approved range.
  • Discuss performance discipline: preparation, controlled execution, and stopping when instructed.
  • Students practise an age-appropriate precision challenge selected by the coach.

Games & application

  • Creative Target Challenge: hit large, fixed, coach-approved novelty targets from an appropriate distance.
  • Precision Demonstration: the instructor demonstrates a suitable skill-based shot only after a separate safety check.
  • Student Challenge: students attempt a simplified, stationary-target version; no hazardous trick techniques.

Learning outcome: Students experience creative archery while recognising that safety and control take priority over spectacle.

Special safety condition: No shots toward or over people, no human-held targets, no blindfold shooting, no shots behind the archer, no fire / breakable / explosive props, and no trick that places a person in the arrow path. Any advanced demonstration is instructor-only and requires prior school approval and a separate risk assessment.

Safety & Supervision Guidelines

  • Archery is conducted only in a designated area that has been inspected and approved by the school.
  • The coach establishes and controls the shooting line, waiting line, target area, and safe retrieval procedure.
  • Students must never point a bow at another person, even when no arrow is nocked.
  • No student may nock or release an arrow until the coach gives explicit permission.
  • All shooting must be directed toward approved targets with suitable backstop and overshoot containment.
  • Students remain behind the waiting line until the coach gives the signal to approach targets.
  • Arrows are retrieved only after all bows are down, the range is declared clear, and the coach gives permission.
  • Equipment is inspected before each session; damaged bows, arrows, strings, or protective gear are removed from use.
  • The number of active shooters and the coach-to-student ratio must be appropriate to the facility, age group, equipment, and school policy.
  • Flip-flop targets must be locked and stationary before shooting; target changes occur only while the range is closed.
  • Spinning and moving target activities require purpose-built equipment, a documented risk assessment, school approval, and qualified instructor control. A static or non-shooting alternative is used if any control is unavailable.
  • Trickshoot activities must not involve people in the arrow path, human-held targets, blindfold shooting, shooting over people, or hazardous props. Advanced demonstrations are instructor-only and separately risk-assessed.
  • The coach stops activity immediately if an unsafe condition, equipment fault, or unauthorised person enters the controlled area.
